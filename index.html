How this works

Height between two sighted points at the same horizontal distance D:

H = D × |tan(top) − tan(base)|

The reference object's known height gives D. That same D, applied to the target's own top angle (sighted from the same base), gives the target's height:

D = H_ref / |tan(α1) − tan(β)|

H_target = D × |tan(α2) − tan(β)|

Once you calibrate, the live number in the viewfinder (labeled "incline") is your elevation angle from horizontal, updating in real time — you can use it as a standalone clinometer just by calibrating and reading that number, without running the rest of the height workflow.

The tilt readout's "vs vertical" hint assumes the phone is held upright in portrait, camera on the back pointing at the target — that position reads about 90° on most phones' tilt sensor when truly plumb. It's a guide for calibrating, not a substitute for a real level reference.

Accuracy depends on how precisely you aim the crosshair and how level your calibration was. Larger reference heights and shorter distances reduce angle-reading error.
